# Vehicle dynamics expansion — 5 November 2026

Implemented in exploration; opt-in controls preserve the established campaign and starter experience.

- **Systems → Thermal & structural failure:** lumped radiative balance, Earth-air entry heating, shield ablation, pressure/acceleration overload and integrity failure. Assumed 8 m² area, 180 kJ/K heat capacity, emissivity 0.8, 120 kg shield, 900 K ablation onset and 25 MJ/kg ablation energy. Pressure overload starts at 60 kPa, acceleration overload at 12 g. These are illustrative material assumptions, not flight-qualified properties. Other atmospheres apply pressure damage but have no calibrated heating law. Ablation reduces the effective propulsion mass. Failure stops thrust and produces eight equal-mass fragments with paired momentum-conserving impulses.
- **Fleet → Vessel collision damage:** swept bounding-sphere tests prevent tunnelling between free vessels. Detached fragments can strike surviving vessels while another vessel is flown. The contact model uses spheres, not detailed mesh collision, fracture mechanics or an elastic impact solver. Collision mode caps requested world advancement at ten seconds. Fragment propagation is a bounded local approximation, not a debris-catalogue propagator.
- **Fleet → Port geometry docking:** opposed nose axes within 5°, port separation ≤0.5 m, lateral error ≤0.2 m and relative speed ≤0.1 m/s. Ports sit 3 m from each craft centre. Attached positions retain that offset; undocking separates along the port axis. Manual alignment is required. Rotational inertia, flexible joints and six-degree-of-freedom contact dynamics remain absent.
- **Systems → JPL DE421:** bundled hourly heliocentric ECLIPJ2000 position/velocity samples for January 2000, interpolated with cubic Hermite polynomials. Earth, Moon, Mercury, Venus and Mars use centres; outer planets use system barycentres. Uncovered bodies retain explicitly approximate Kepler paths. Data comes from the DE421 kernel distributed by skyfield-data 7.0.0; the original kernel SHA-256 and sampling configuration are in `data/ephemerides/de421-provenance.json`. Twenty-seven held-out half-hour samples have position error below 2 m against direct kernel evaluation. This does not establish 2 m agreement with observations. Epoch is TDB seconds from JD2451545; precision time-scale/Earth-orientation conversion remains outside this model. Out-of-window requests are rejected. The pack adds approximately 370 kB compressed to the browser and worker bundles. No runtime server is used.
- **Systems → Degree 3 + lagged elastic tide:** adds degree-three response with assumed k₃=0.093 and an optional 0–600 s body-rotating constant time lag. The Earth tide switch must also be enabled. Degree three and lag are experimentally selectable; these are not an IERS frequency-dependent tide solution or ocean-loading implementation.

The new thermal, collision and docking options are educational approximations. Full ocean-loading data, calibrated spacecraft materials, detailed fracture/contact mechanics and longer/current ephemeris packs remain unfinished; this document does not classify those as implemented.

Regeneration: install `skyfield-data==7.0.0`, `jplephem==2.24` in a development environment and run `scripts/reference/generate_ephemeris.py` with its documented PYTHONPATH. The browser only needs bundled JSON. Original package attribution is preserved in `data/ephemerides/LICENSE-skyfield-data.txt`.
