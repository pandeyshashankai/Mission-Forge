01 · The problem and the solution

Altitude alone does not make an orbit.

A learner should be able to explain why a rocket falls back—and what to change next.

Predict→Build & fly→Inspect evidence→Reflect & retry

Retain the prediction

Record an explanation and a speed estimate before the showcase. Wrong predictions remain visible.

Explain the actual flight

Compare periapsis and recorded telemetry with the ideal reference. Save a reflection and export the learning record.

Educational effectiveness is a hypothesis to test with learners; no learning-gain study is claimed.

02 · NASA data that changes the simulation

From a source field to a flight decision.

12

Body radii

JPL/NAIF pck00011 triaxial axes converted into volume-equivalent spheres.

10

Gravity parameters

NASA GMAT defaults converted from km³/s² to m³/s². Europa/Titan gravity is separately labelled.

178

Earth/Mars density bands

Eight preserved NASA data/method snapshots, including EGM96 gravity, plus 12 signed NAIF rotation periods, with pinned provenance and checksums.

Radius controls altitude and the surface boundary. μ controls gravity and orbital speed. The in-game panel exposes both values and their source fields.

Verified channel: NASA’s official nasa/GMAT repository. Archived defaults, not live ephemerides or NASA endorsement.
03 · Science with inspectable limits

Calculate independently. Then test the integrator.

Ideal circular speed v = √(μ/r)
Period T = 2π√(r³/μ)

1,634 m/s

Moon · 100 km

A different gravity well changes the flight requirement. The same formula provides a useful comparison.

Independent references: Decimal equations, actual Orekit trajectories and gravity vectors, native-C atmosphere cases, and a published ERFA frame matrix. Inputs, forces and tolerances are exposed alongside results.

These are ideal two-body numerical checks. They do not validate all atmospheres, ephemerides, re-entry conditions, or learning outcomes.

04 · Implementation and reliability

A complete mission, across touch and keyboard.

Build & fly

Four scored missions, solar exploration, local fleets/docking, programmable controls and custom worlds.

Accessible controls

WASD defaults, remapping, independent touch steering/throttle, stage and RCS buttons, mirrored layouts and prepared offline play.

Reproducible evidence

321 regression checks; 12 campaign validations; 51 external-reference checks; replay, verified local backups, source checksums and complete browser showcase checks.

NASA gravity changes exposed an overly long lunar-return solution. The planner now checks return time, and the crewed sequence is validated through recovery.

The stock Expert Lunar Orbiter remains a budget challenge with expected partial success. Mobile tests are emulated; physical-device testing remains.
05 · Live showcase

One question. One dependable flight.

  1. Show NASA inputs: compare Earth and the Moon and run the science checks.
  2. Save a prediction: what keeps the spacecraft in orbit, and how fast must it travel?
  3. Launch First Orbit: use the tested starter, guidance, and ×10 time speed.
  4. Inspect periapsis: show the difference between reaching space and staying in orbit.
  5. Explain and export: compare the recorded flight, save a reflection, and open the learning journal.
